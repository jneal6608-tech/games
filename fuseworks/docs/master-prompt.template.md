## 2. The product in one page

**Fuseworks** is a portrait, one-thumb, turn-based puzzle roguelite about building a firework display.

- **The hook:** place firework shells on a night-sky grid, light **one** fuse, and watch the whole sky chain-react. Every round ends in a spectacular, filmable payoff.
- **The loop:** your display stays in the sky. Each Match you add more shells and the whole thing fires again, bigger. Applause = Sparkle x Awe. Beat the applause target before you run out of Matches.
- **The depth:** between shows you buy **charms** that bend the scoring rules, add or refine shells, and trim your crate, exactly the "break the game with a build" pleasure that score-builder roguelikes made popular.
- **Session length:** one show takes 1 to 3 minutes; a full festival run takes 25 to 40 minutes; the Daily Show takes about 5 minutes.
- **Audience:** players of puzzle and roguelike deckbuilder games, 13+. Not directed at children.
- **Platform:** Android phones, Android 7.0 (API 24) and newer, target API 36. Works fully offline.
- **Business model (approved by the owner):** free download. **No forced ads, ever.** Three opt-in rewarded-ad placements, plus a one-time **Premium** purchase (about US$3.99) that removes ads and grants those rewards automatically.
- **Why this game:** hybrid-casual puzzle is the growing mobile segment while match-3 and merge are saturated; players are loudly tired of forced ads; the visuals are 100% procedural (zero art budget); and the turn-based rules can be auto-tested and auto-balanced by an AI agent.

### 3.4 A show, step by step
1. The sky is generated from the run seed: star cells and blocked cloud cells are placed, the Finale's weather is chosen, the crate is shuffled and the hand is dealt.
2. **Build:** the player moves any shells from hand to empty cells. Shells placed this turn may still be moved or returned to the hand.
3. **Aim:** the player taps any shell on the sky. The game previews the chain: dashed links between shells and a numbered badge showing firing order, plus a count of bursts and of shells left unlit.
4. **Light:** the player presses *Light fuse*. The chain resolves (3.5) and animates (4.4). The Match is spent.
5. **Everything stays:** every shell on the sky becomes **locked**, a permanent part of the display for this show. The hand refills.
6. Repeat from step 2. Later Matches re-fire the whole growing display, so scores escalate sharply: a typical show goes roughly 300, then 1,500, then 5,000.
7. The show is **cleared** the moment total applause reaches the target (unused Matches pay bonus coins). If Matches run out first, the show **fails** (3.8).

### 3.5 Chain resolution (exact algorithm)
Input: the sky and the cell the player lit. Output: an ordered list of bursts and the score.

```
Sparkle = 0; Awe = 1; order = 0
queue = [lit cell]            // breadth-first
visited = {lit cell}
while queue not empty:
    cell = queue.pop_front(); shell = cell.shell
    repeats = 1
            + 1 if shell is a Strobe
            + 1 if cell is a Twin Star (2 with the Stargazer charm)
            + 1 if this is the lit cell and the player holds Echo
    for rep in 0 .. repeats-1:
        order += 1
        dSparkle = shell Sparkle (+ refine levels), with all step bonuses below
        dAwe     = (1 if order > 1) + all step bonuses below
        if rep == 0:
            for each cell in the shell's burst pattern, in table order:
                skip if out of bounds, blocked, or the cell itself
                if it holds a shell not yet visited: mark visited, remember this cell as its parent, push to queue
        Sparkle += dSparkle; Awe += dAwe; record the burst
apply end-of-chain additive Awe (Showstopper, Slow Fuse, Piggy Bank) to the last burst
finalAwe = Awe x every applicable multiplier charm, rounded to 1 decimal
applause = round(Sparkle x finalAwe)
```

Step bonuses, applied to each burst:
- Kamuro Sparkle is `4 x order` instead of a fixed value.
- Drizzle weather: minus 4 Sparkle (never below 0).
- Bright Star: +25 Sparkle. Awe Star: +2 Awe. Both doubled by Stargazer.
- **Harmony:** on a shell's first burst, if the shell that triggered it (its parent) has the same colour: +1 Awe (+3 with Choir; nothing in Tough Crowd weather).
- Glitter shell: +1 Awe.
- Charm bonuses exactly as worded in the charm table.

Rules that must hold: patterns are **directional** (a Comet reaches up, so the shell above it is triggered, not the reverse); a shell is triggered at most once per Match; shells the chain never reaches score nothing and are reported as "unlit"; Crosswind weather adds +1 to the column of every pattern offset.

## 4. Visual direction

**Look:** a deep navy night sky, soft horizon glow, tiny stars, a black silhouette skyline. Everything luminous is drawn with additive blending. Interface panels are dark translucent glass with thin light borders; primary actions use a warm orange-to-pink gradient; coins and rewards are gold; Sparkle is cyan; Awe is violet. The feeling is calm anticipation, then spectacle.

**No image files.** All art is drawn by code at runtime, and icons and store graphics are generated by a script. This keeps the download tiny, avoids all licensing, and makes every asset editable in code.

### 4.1 Layout (portrait only)
- Top HUD: menu button, night and show name, coins; an applause meter (score / target) that turns green when full; a large `Sparkle x Awe` readout; a row with the weather chip and the 5 charm slots.
- Centre: the 5 x 6 sky grid, sized to fit between HUD and tray, cells at most 92 px.
- Bottom tray: a one-line context hint, the hand as cards (icon, name, Sparkle), and three buttons: Restock (with count), Crate (with count), and the large *Light fuse* button showing Matches left.
- Respect display cut-outs and gesture bars using safe-area insets.

### 4.2 Shell tokens
Each shell is a dark token whose **outline shape encodes colour** and whose face is a **miniature diagram of its burst pattern**: a white centre dot and coloured dots at the offsets it reaches (long-reach shells add short rays). Small badges mark behaviours a diagram cannot show (`x2` Strobe, `#` Kamuro, `+1` Glitter). Gold pips under the token show its refine level. Shells placed this turn have a marching dashed outline (still movable); locked shells have a solid glowing outline.

### 4.3 Cells
Rounded squares with a faint fill. Star cells show a pulsing gold four-point star (Bright), violet heart (Awe) or twin cyan rings (Twin). Blocked cells show a grey cloud. Highlights: valid drop cells pulse softly while a shell is held; the cells a dragged shell would reach are tinted cyan; the selected shell's reach is tinted gold.

### 4.4 The chain animation (the heart of the game)
- A fuse hiss, then bursts fire **one at a time in chain order**, starting about 270 ms apart and accelerating by 7% per burst to a floor of 75 ms.
- Each burst: a flash, an expanding ring, 30 to 50 glowing particles in a style unique to the shell type (Ring = perfect circle, Willow = drooping gold trails, Comet = upward stream, Crossette = four diagonal jets, Salute = white flash with screen shake, and so on), plus bright "fuse sparks" that race to every cell the burst reaches so cause and effect are readable.
- Floating `+Sparkle` text in the shell's colour, and a violet label for Awe events ("Harmony").
- The HUD readout ticks up with a pop on every burst. Multiplier charms then slam in one by one as banners ("x2 Awe, Grand Finale"). Finally `+applause` slams in, the crowd cheers, and the meter fills.
- Tapping during the animation fast-forwards it four times faster.
- Cap live particles (about 1,700; about 500 in Reduced Effects mode) and automatically thin particles if the frame rate drops.

## 5. Audio

**All sound is synthesised at runtime with the Web Audio API.** No audio files.

- **Bursts:** filtered-noise boom with a short reverb tail, a low sine thump, a few high crackles, and a soft tone whose pitch climbs a **major pentatonic ladder with the chain order**, so long chains play a rising melody. Larger shells boom deeper.
- **Other effects:** select, place, pick up, deny, restock rustle, fuse hiss, Harmony chime, multiplier chord stab, score-count ticks, crowd cheer scaled to how much of the target the Match scored, show-cleared arpeggio, failure phrase, coin, purchase, unlock fanfare.
- **Music:** a slow generative ambient loop: four-chord cycle (about 6.4 s per chord) of detuned triangle pads through a low-pass filter, a soft sine bass, and sparse pentatonic plucks into the reverb. It must sit well under the effects.
- Audio starts on the first touch (browser policy), suspends when the app is backgrounded, and has separate Music and Sound toggles. Use a compressor on the master bus so big chains never clip.
- **Haptics:** light tick per burst, heavy for large shells, success and failure patterns; toggle in settings.

## 6. Interaction rules

- **Place:** drag a hand card onto a cell (the shell floats about 46 px above the finger so it stays visible, and the cells it would reach light up), **or** tap a card then tap a cell. Both must work.
- **Adjust:** before lighting, drag a just-placed shell to another cell, or drag it back onto the tray to return it to the hand. Locked shells cannot move.
- **Aim and light:** tap a shell to select it and preview the chain; tap again to deselect. *Light fuse* pulses when a start is selected. Pressing it with nothing selected shows a hint and does nothing.
- **Inspect:** tapping a charm or the weather chip opens a sheet with its rule (and Sell, for charms).
- Every touch target is at least 44 px. The Android back button closes the top sheet, then opens the pause menu in a run, then leaves sub-screens, then exits from the title.
- The game autosaves after every action and when backgrounded. Killing the app at any moment, including mid-animation, must never lose or duplicate progress: the engine state is saved **before** the animation plays.

## 7. Technical architecture

### 7.1 Stack (use this unless your environment makes it impossible; if so, ask)
- **TypeScript + Vite**, no UI framework. One full-screen `<canvas>` (2D context) for sky, board and particles; plain DOM + CSS over it for HUD, menus and sheets.
- **Capacitor 8** to package for Android, with `@capacitor/app`, `haptics`, `preferences`, `share`, `splash-screen`, `status-bar`; `@capacitor-community/admob` for rewarded ads and the consent flow; `@capgo/native-purchases` for Google Play Billing.
- **Vitest** for unit tests, **Playwright** (Chromium) for end-to-end tests, `tsx` for scripts.

Why this stack: the game is a turn-based, text-heavy 2D grid game, which web technology renders well; menus are far faster to build in HTML/CSS; and, decisively, **an AI agent can run, see, test and build it in a headless Linux environment**, which is not true of editor-centric engines. The rules engine is pure data and functions, so a port to another engine later is cheap if ever justified.

### 7.3 Rules for the code
- The engine exposes plain functions that mutate a plain-JSON `RunState`. `resolve()` computes a chain without changing state (used for the preview and by bots); `ignite()` applies it and returns the same `Resolution` for the UI to animate.
- The UI never computes scores. It only calls the engine and plays back the result.
- The whole run is one JSON object: saving is `JSON.stringify`, resuming is `JSON.parse`.
- Saves are versioned. Loading passes through a migration function that fills missing fields and discards unknown IDs, so an update never breaks an existing player's save.
- Storage: Capacitor Preferences on Android, `localStorage` in the browser. All platform calls go through `platform.ts` and must fail soft (a storage or network error never crashes or blocks the game).
- In the browser build, ads and purchases are replaced by clearly labelled test dialogs so every reward path can be exercised without a phone.

## 8. Monetisation implementation

**Principle: the player is never interrupted.** No banners, no interstitials, no timers, no energy, no loot boxes. The only purchasable currency is tickets (below), which only speed up permanent unlocks and never buy power inside a run or random rewards.

Three rewarded-ad placements, each a button the player chooses to press:
1. **Encore** after a failed show: one extra Match, once per run.
2. **Double tickets** on the end-of-run screen.
3. **Free reroll** once per Market visit.

Rules: grant the reward only when the ad network reports the reward was earned; closing early grants nothing and returns the player to where they were; if no ad is available, say so politely and grant nothing; preload the next ad after each one. Run Google's consent flow (UMP) at startup and expose "Ad privacy choices" in settings.

**Ticket packs** (consumable, approved by the owner): `tickets_60` 60 tickets about US$0.99, `tickets_200` 200 tickets about US$2.99 (tagged Popular), `tickets_500` 500 tickets about US$5.99 (tagged Best value). Reached from a `+` on the ticket counter on the title and Workshop screens, and offered automatically when the player taps an unlock they cannot afford. Show localised prices; grant the tickets only after Play confirms the purchase; never show the shop unprompted. **Purchase safety (required):** turn off the billing library's automatic finishing; save the reward first, then consume (packs) or acknowledge (Premium); record each purchase token so it pays out once; at launch, on resume and when the ticket shop opens, query owned purchases and deliver any paid but undelivered ones; treat pending payments as pending (tell the player, deliver when they clear). Unit-test this delivery logic as a pure function.

**Premium** (one-time, non-consumable, product ID `premium_unlock`, about US$3.99): no ads at all; all three rewards granted instantly without an ad; the Aurora sky. Show the localised store price. Check ownership at startup and offer "Restore purchase" in settings so a reinstall or new phone keeps Premium. Erasing progress must not erase Premium.

Ship with **Google's public test ad IDs** and test mode on. Put every ID in `config.ts` and the AdMob App ID in Android `strings.xml`, with comments saying what to replace. Never hard-code real IDs you were not given.
