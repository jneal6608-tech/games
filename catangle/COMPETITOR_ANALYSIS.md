# Competitor analysis

Install and revenue figures are third-party tracker estimates found on 11 November 2026.

## Closest competitors

| Game | Who | What it is | Evidence of success | Weakness we use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Arrows Puzzle Escape | Large publisher | Abstract arrows on a grid; tap to slide out | Reported $6.6M US ad revenue in one month; top download charts | No characters, no collection, heavy ad load |
| Arrow Escape: Maze Puzzle Out | Studio about 6 months old | Same mechanic | 500K+ installs | Generic look; nothing to remember it by |
| Arrow Escape: Logic Puzzle, Arrowdom and similar | Small studios | Same mechanic | 100K to 1M installs each | Interchangeable; compete only on store keywords |
| Longcat: cat puzzle | Solo developer | Stretch-a-cat slide puzzle (different mechanic) | About 850K installs, 4.5 rating | Shows the cat theme pulls installs; not an escape puzzle |
| Stretched Cats | Small studio | Tap the uncovered cat | Listed on both stores | Simple depth; proves the theme is being tried |
| Magic Sort, Knit Out, Pixel Flow | Mid-size publishers | Sort puzzles | Sort subgenre about $280M in 2025 | Different mechanic; they outspend us on ads |
| Screwdom, Color Block Jam | Publishers | Screw and block leaders | 45% and 75% of their subgenres | Do not enter their lanes |

## Why the leaders earn money

1. **A one-tap rule anyone understands in two seconds.** No tutorial wall.
2. **Levels that take one to three minutes**, so there are many natural ad breaks per session.
3. **Fail states that sell a rewarded video** (extra life, hint) at the moment the player most wants it.
4. **Endless level counts**, produced cheaply by generators.
5. **Store search.** Players search "arrow puzzle" or "escape puzzle" and install whatever looks good.

## Why a player would pick Catangle instead

| Their game | Catangle |
|---|---|
| Arrows and lines | Cats with faces, moods and sound. A bonk is funny instead of annoying. |
| Nothing to collect | 16 coats and 4 rooms that change how every level looks. |
| One rule forever | Sleepy cats (order matters) and grumpy cats (a countdown) add planning from level 15 and 30. |
| No reason to return tomorrow | Daily Tangle with a growing streak reward. |
| An ad after most levels | No ads for the first 11 levels, then at most one every three wins and never within 150 seconds. |
| Hard to tell apart in a video | A screen of tangled cats is recognisable in a silent two-second clip. |

## Honest view

The mechanic is not new and we do not claim it is. The bet is that theme, polish and lighter ads win store-search installs and short-form attention in a subgenre where nobody has built a brand yet. If a large publisher ships a cat version with a marketing budget, our edge narrows to quality and ad load.
