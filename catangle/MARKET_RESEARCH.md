# Market research: Project 03

Researched 11 November 2026. Figures come from trade coverage of Sensor Tower, AppMagic, Naavik and GameAnalytics reports, not from the paid reports themselves. Treat exact numbers as directional. Sources are listed at the end.

## 1. The market in one page

| Signal | Finding |
|---|---|
| Overall size | Mobile game spending was about $81.8B in 2025. H1 2026: in-app revenue about $40B (down 2%), downloads 24B (down 12%), ad spend up 8%. |
| What is growing | Puzzle is the standout: about $4.9B in H1 2026, 44% of casual spending, more than RPG and strategy combined. Hybrid-casual was the only segment with meaningful in-app growth (about +20% a year). |
| What is shrinking | Mid-core (strategy, RPG) keeps losing ground. Blast puzzles have declined for three years. Match-3 is flat at just under $5B a year. |
| Where puzzle growth comes from | Merge-2 drove about 60% of puzzle's growth. The other 40% came from newer subgenres: sort, screw and block, together about $0.7B and up 222% in a year. |
| Sort | About $280M in 2025, up 176%. Overtook blast in early 2026. Earns at least as much from ads as from purchases. |
| Block | Winner-takes-all: one title (Color Block Jam) holds about 75% of subgenre revenue. |
| Screw | Best retention (one leader at about 17% day-30 in the US) but one title holds about 45% of revenue. |
| Arrow-escape ("tap away") | Among the top download charts in 2026. One publisher's title was reported at $6.6M US ad revenue in a month. Several studios under a year old show 100K to 500K+ installs on near-identical games. |
| Retention benchmarks | Puzzle: roughly 32% day-1, 12% day-7, 5% day-30. Hyper-casual: 29% / 6% / 1.4%. Medians fell again in 2025. |
| Ad rates | Android rewarded video in the US: roughly $13 to $30 per thousand views depending on source. Europe about $9, Latin America about $3. |
| Revenue per daily player | Ads-only casual: $0.01 to $0.05. Hybrid-casual: $0.15 to $0.50. |

## 2. What this means for a small developer

1. **A big genre is not an opportunity by itself.** Strategy and match-3 earn the most and are the worst places to enter: revenue sits with a few publishers that buy players at a scale we cannot match.
2. **Ad-funded puzzle subgenres are the realistic entry point.** They need no live-ops team and no paid marketing to reach first revenue, and the top of the genre proves players exist.
3. **Arrow-escape is the least consolidated of the growing subgenres.** No single title owns it the way one owns block or screw, and new small studios are still getting installs from store search.
4. **Most arrow-escape games look the same**: abstract arrows on a white grid. That is the opening for a branded, characterful take.
5. **Downloads are falling market-wide**, so store search and short-form video matter more than chart position. A game has to be recognisable in a two-second clip.

## 4. Decision

**Winner: a cat-themed arrow-escape puzzle, built as Catangle.** It has the best mix of proven demand, low build risk, open competition and organic reach. Sort scores second but its leaders spend heavily on ads. Merge-2 and 4X have higher ceilings and are out of reach for our scope.

Your first game was a puzzle. The research still points to puzzle, in a different and newer subgenre, so I did not avoid it.
